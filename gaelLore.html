His Mission — The Painter and the Dark Soul

By the time we meet Gael, he serves one purpose: to find the pigment of the Dark Soul so that a young Painting Girl (a child painter) can create a new painted world — a world to succeed Ariandel, which has rotted and been burned away. A painted world is literally born from a painting, and the only pigment worthy of creating such a world is the blood of the Dark Soul — the essence of humanity itself, the primordial darkness that predates even the gods. Gael becomes the Painting Girl's uncle figure, her protector, and ultimately her would-be savior.

Searching Across the End of the World

Gael travels to the Ringed City — the oldest city in existence, built at the literal end of the world and the end of time — because that is where the pygmies (the original inheritors of the Dark Soul) reside, sealed away by the gods. He needs their Dark Soul blood. But Gael is human. And human blood cannot contain the Dark Soul in its full, undiluted power.

Gael's Tragic Downfall

In his desperation and love for the Painting Girl, Gael consumed the Dark Souls of the pygmies himself — drinking their dark blood to gather the pigment. But a normal human body cannot hold such ancient, boundless power. The Dark Soul consumed him from the inside, driving him completely and utterly mad. By the time we find him in the Ringed City DLC, Gael has been wandering the ashen wasteland at the end of all things for an unknowable amount of time — possibly centuries. He is no longer the loyal, gentle knight he once was. He is a monstrous, desperate, half-mad creature, blood-soaked and raving, driven only by the faintest remnant of his original purpose.

The Boss Fight

We fight Gael at the very End of the World — a ash-covered desert at the edge of time, under a blood-red sky, with the ruins of civilization crumbled to nothing around you. It is one of the most desolate and beautiful arenas in the entire series. Gael fights in three savage phases. He is fast, brutal, and unpredictable — using his massive red cape, a crossbow, and a greatsword with dark soul energy crackling through it. He calls down lightning, drags his sword through the earth, and launches himself across the arena. He feels utterly unstoppable, like something that has been fighting and surviving for eons. He is not fighting you out of malice. Deep down, beneath the madness, the last flicker of his purpose remains: he knows the Painting Girl needs this blood. He knows he cannot simply give it. He fights you because he has to be stopped — his blood can only be taken when he falls.

After the Fight

When you kill Gael, his blood — now saturated with the essence of the Dark Soul after he consumed the pygmies — is available as the pigment. You bring it back to the Painting Girl. She is finally able to paint a new world: a cold, dark place without fire, without the cycle of linking flames, a world made for humanity. Gael dies achieving everything he set out to do, but at the cost of his mind, his humanity, and ultimately his life. He was consumed by the very thing he sought to obtain, destroyed by an act of love for a child who only wanted to create something beautiful.

Why Gael is so beloved

His arc is a perfect encapsulation of Dark Souls' themes — sacrifice, the horror of loving too deeply in a dying world, and the tragedy of good intentions corrupted by forces too large for any individual to contain. His boss fight is considered by many to be the greatest in the Soulsborne series, and his story is a farewell not just to his character, but to the entire Age of Fire.
